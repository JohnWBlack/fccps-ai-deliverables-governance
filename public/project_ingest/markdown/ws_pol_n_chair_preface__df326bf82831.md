WS-POL-N chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should account for the budgeting, staffing, workload, and implementation-capacity implications of AI policy and practice. The Committee broadly agrees that these issues are not secondary operational details, but practical constraints that shape what policy can responsibly require. The Committee also broadly agrees that AI may reduce some burdens in planning, drafting, or administrative support, while also creating new demands in training, review, compliance, monitoring, and communication. Any policy that assumes unlimited staff capacity or ignores the real burden of implementation risks becoming unsustainable in practice.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on the balance between efficiency gains and burden shifting. One position emphasizes that AI may help relieve teacher workload and support more cost-effective delivery of some educational practices, provided human judgment remains primary and use is well governed. Another is more skeptical, warning that apparent efficiencies may be offset by new obligations such as training, tool review, disclosure, assessment redesign, vendor oversight, and compliance monitoring, and that these added demands may fall unevenly on teachers and staff. Members also differ on how much this section should speak directly to staffing levels versus focusing more narrowly on workload protection, implementation burden, and district capacity.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should not adopt AI expectations that exceed the district’s real capacity to train, support, review, and govern them. The remaining disagreement is mainly about whether AI should be framed more as a tool for bounded efficiency gains or more as a source of new workload, compliance, and staffing pressures that require caution and tighter scoping.
