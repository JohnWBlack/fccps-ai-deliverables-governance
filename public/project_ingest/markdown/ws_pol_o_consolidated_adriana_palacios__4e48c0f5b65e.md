WS-POL-O – Reassessment and Updating of Policy

The committee recommends that AI policy be adaptive and regularly updated in response to evolving technology, emerging risks, and observed outcomes in practice. AI policy should not be treated as static. Instead, the district should establish a clear and predictable approach for reviewing new uses, evaluating impacts, responding to incidents, and revising policy and guidance over time.

The committee recognizes that AI capabilities are developing rapidly and that students and staff are already interacting with these tools both within and outside of school environments. As a result, policy reassessment should support both risk mitigation and the district’s responsibility to prepare students to use AI tools thoughtfully and responsibly.

To support effective governance, the committee recommends a structured but flexible approach to reassessment. Review processes should be stable enough to support trust and consistent implementation, while remaining responsive to new evidence, technological change, and classroom realities. The district should define how AI uses are evaluated over time, what outcomes or incidents trigger review, and how feedback from students, families, and staff informs updates.

The committee recommends that new or higher-risk uses of AI begin as bounded pilots rather than immediate system-wide implementation. Pilot programs should include clearly defined goals, appropriate safeguards, and limits on scope and duration. Piloting should serve not only to identify risks, but also to better understand how AI can support learning, efficiency, and access.

The committee further recommends that the district establish clear criteria for evaluating the effectiveness and impact of AI use. Evaluation should consider anticipated benefits, baseline conditions prior to implementation, and outcomes over time. It should also include attention to unintended effects, including impacts on student learning, equity, and staff workload.

The committee emphasizes the importance of clear processes for responding to incidents involving AI tools. These may include issues related to academic integrity, privacy or data exposure, or harmful or inappropriate outputs affecting students. Response processes should include defined escalation pathways, clear responsibility for review and action, and timely communication where appropriate. In all cases, incident response should prioritize student well-being, fairness, and transparency.

The committee recommends that implementation occur in phases, recognizing that some elements of AI use can begin immediately while others require additional infrastructure, training, or evaluation. A phased approach supports thoughtful adoption and reduces disruption, allowing the district to sequence more complex implementations over time.

The committee also recommends establishing a regular review cadence for AI policy and related guidance. This may include periodic administrative review, School Board review at defined intervals, and accelerated review when triggered by significant incidents, technological developments, or new evidence. Review processes should be predictable, while allowing flexibility when needed.

Ongoing feedback should be an integral part of policy reassessment. Input from students, families, and staff should inform continuous improvement, alongside observed classroom outcomes, implementation challenges, and emerging risks or opportunities. Feedback mechanisms described in related sections (e.g., communication and feedback processes) should be integrated into reassessment efforts.

Finally, the committee recognizes that continuous reassessment must be balanced with the need for clarity and stability. Frequent or poorly communicated changes may create confusion, while delayed updates may limit the district’s ability to respond effectively. The policy should therefore establish a clear framework for reassessment that supports timely, practical updates without undermining consistency.
