# WS POL B chair preface

Here is a draft WS-POL-B chair preface in the same form we used for WS-POL-A.

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses one of the clearest policy tradeoffs identified by the Committee: how FCCPS should balance protecting student learning and assessment integrity against enabling thoughtful, developmentally appropriate integration of AI to support student preparation and AI literacy. The Committee broadly agrees that policy must protect learning, fairness, trust, and student well-being, and that FCCPS should avoid both unchecked adoption and impractical overrestriction. The Committee also agrees that any policy in this area must account for the practical difficulty of enforcing AI restrictions in unproctored settings, the importance of preserving trust between students and teachers, and the risk of unfair outcomes, including false accusations and uneven compliance.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on default posture. One position places greater weight on restricting AI use unless clear educational benefit can be shown and risks are well controlled, emphasizing that current evidence of benefit is uneven while harms to learning and independent thinking are already visible. Another position places greater weight on guided, well-governed integration, emphasizing that AI is already widely accessible outside school, that restriction-first policies may be difficult to enforce consistently, and that FCCPS should prepare students to use AI thoughtfully, transparently, and responsibly. A middle view emphasizes that the Board should understand this as a genuine policy tradeoff rather than a disagreement over whether learning protection matters.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should not normalize AI use without guardrails, but it does not agree on whether policy should begin from stronger presumptive restrictions or from guided integration with targeted limits. The disagreement is best understood as a difference in policy posture under uncertainty: how to protect learning and integrity while also responding to the realities of student access, enforceability, and long-term preparation.
