WS-POL-M chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should understand and address equity across the full lifecycle of AI use in the district, including access to tools, instructional use, assessment practices, and the possibility that unequal access outside school may create unintended advantages or disadvantages for students. The Committee broadly agrees that equity should not be treated as a standalone afterthought, but should inform decisions from procurement through classroom implementation and ongoing review. The Committee also broadly agrees that FCCPS should monitor for disparate impact, algorithmic bias where relevant, and uneven implementation effects across students, families, and schools.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on what equity most strongly requires in practice. One position emphasizes supported access, arguing that FCCPS should reduce inequities in AI fluency and opportunity by ensuring students are not disadvantaged simply because they lack outside access, guidance, or approved tools. Another emphasizes the risks of overreliance, warning that expanding AI use without strong guardrails may deepen existing inequities, create new forms of dependence, or place some students at a disadvantage if they prefer or need a meaningful non-AI path. Members also differ on how much of the equity framework should be expressed directly in policy language versus carried through guidance, monitoring tools, procurement review, and implementation structures.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should evaluate AI policy and practice through an equity lens from the beginning and should monitor whether AI implementation advantages some students over others. The remaining disagreement is mainly about posture: whether equity is best advanced primarily through broader supported access, through stronger limits on overreliance and coercive AI norms, or through some combination of both.
