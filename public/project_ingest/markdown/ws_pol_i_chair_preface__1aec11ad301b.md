WS-POL-I chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should define expectations for citation, disclosure, and process evidence when students use AI in schoolwork. The Committee broadly agrees that transparency around meaningful AI use is important to support learning integrity, fairness, and trust. The Committee also broadly agrees that expectations should be understandable to students, aligned to assignment goals, and developmentally appropriate. At the same time, members recognize that disclosure and process-evidence requirements can create tradeoffs involving privacy, practicality, teacher workload, and the risk of over-standardizing diverse classroom practices.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how much should be disclosed, when disclosure should be required, and whether process evidence should be expected routinely or only in selected contexts. One position favors stronger disclosure expectations and is skeptical of flexible or context-dependent standards that may create ambiguity or inconsistent enforcement. Another favors grade-banded, assignment-specific expectations that focus on meaningful AI assistance and use process evidence as part of assessment design rather than as a universal compliance requirement. Members also differ on whether process evidence such as drafts, checkpoints, reflections, or chat logs is best understood as a tool for preserving learning visibility or as an intrusive and potentially burdensome requirement that may be easy to game.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that students should not hide meaningful AI assistance and that FCCPS should set clearer expectations for transparency. The remaining disagreement is mainly about scope and mechanism: whether disclosure should be broad or more contextual, and whether process evidence should function as a routine expectation, a targeted instructional tool, or only a limited backstop in selected cases.
