WS-POL-I Citation & Disclosure

With respect to citation, note the following common-sense principles:

Text generated by AI doesn’t qualify as a source and so it shouldn’t be directly cited.

If Generative AI leads students to human sources, those human sources must be cited if the ideas therein are not common knowledge and the student reads and relies on the ideas in those sources for their work.

For citation purposes, reading an AI-generated summary of a document is not the same as actually reading the document. Students shouldn’t cite documents they haven’t actually read.

With respect to disclosure of Generative AI, the Board should recognize that the core of the issue is how much needs to be disclosed to the teacher in order enable fair assessment of the assignment at hand. This may vary depending on the learning objectives, the age of the students, and the subject being taught. In lieu of a comprehensive matrix of all possible scenarios, here are some heuristic principles:

In contexts where highly iterative and interactive reliance on Generative AI is expected and allowed (e.g., certain computer programming assignments) it is impractical to ask students to retain a chat log. For similar reasons, one would not expect students to keep track of minor grammar/style corrections that their word processor automatically suggests to them.

When the assignment is merely formative (i.e., as opposed to summative), teachers may be able to get by with a lesser standard of disclosure from their students regarding their GenAI use on the assignment. Nonetheless, it would be good for teachers to have a general awareness of what AI their students are using on the formatives, to help explain a sudden drop in performance from the formatives to the summative.

In contexts where the teacher has imposed a subtle boundary on permissible Generative AI use (e.g., “you can ask it to review your first draft but you can’t ask it to make the revisions for you”), there is a higher need for detailed student disclosure of what exactly they asked the AI to do. Knowing that one’s chat logs with the AI are accessible to the teacher will help to keep “honest students honest.” Without a chat log, students will be tempted to incrementally and semi-intentionally wander off into prohibited use cases.
