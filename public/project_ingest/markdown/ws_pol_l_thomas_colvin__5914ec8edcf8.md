WS-POL-L – Public Accountability, Notice, Consent, and Feedback

The committee believes that responsible adoption of AI in schools requires strong accountability structures for governance and incident response, plain-language communication about how AI is being used, and effective mechanisms for students, families, and staff to provide feedback when concerns, harms, or new risks emerge.

# Accountability

The policy should create an accountability structure that gives a single high-level official the responsibility for the success or failure of AI in FCCPS. Compared to diffuse responsibility, where no one is clearly accountable, connecting a single person’s reputation to final outcomes creates incentives for better decisions, faster problem recognition, and stronger drives to correct course when needed. The accountable official should have an enterprise-level view of AI-related implementation issues (e.g., learning outcomes, academic integrity, mental health, privacy, procurement, tech support). Second, they should have authority to approve or veto AI-related actions; responsibility without authority is a recipe for failure. Third, they should have sufficient tenure that they will still be in the role when the effects of their decisions come to fruition.

# Consent

The policy should establish a process for identifying the uses of AI that should be consent-based. Even if the use of AI is broadly accepted by the community, some parents will not want their children using AI tools or having AI tools used on their children in any significant capacity. FCCPS should recognize that gathering consent is a mechanism to protect the interests of minority stakeholders in such situations. # Disclosure

The policy should grant parents and students the right to know the role that AI played in critical decisions, such as those contributing to final grades, disciplinary actions, and IEPs. For example, such decisions could be labeled to indicate whether it was produced (A) without any AI involvement, (B) with only non-substantive AI contributions, or (C) with substantive AI contributions. For Category C documents, stakeholders should have the right to inspect the record of AI use that contributed to the decision. This policy would require new retention and request procedures for AI-related documents. This type of disclosure policy encourages accountability within the school and increases community trust.

# Notice and Feedback

The policy should adopt the Rapid-Alert & Re-Assessment Pathway, which is a mechanism for gathering and responding to concerns from teachers, administrators, parents, and students. It was created with support from State Council of Higher Education for Virginia (SCHEV) for use with AI-adoption. It proceeds as follows.

Invite reports from every stakeholder through a one-click online form and a dedicated helpline.

Triage submissions within 48 hours (e.g., data-privacy breach, discriminatory outputs, instructional harm) and posts a public “under review” notice for the tool or issue in question.

Convenes an expert review panel to decide whether to (a) maintain approval, (b) approve with conditions, or (c) suspend clearance pending vendor remediation.

Publishes an outcome summary so that stakeholders have a shared understanding of decision.

Logs all incidents in an open dashboard to spot systemic risks and guide future policy updates.
