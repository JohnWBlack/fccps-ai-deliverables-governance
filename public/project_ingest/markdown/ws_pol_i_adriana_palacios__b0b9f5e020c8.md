# WS-POL-i — Equity in Our Community

## Decision — Committee Perspective (No Full Consensus)

The committee generally agrees that equity must be a central consideration in the use of AI across FCCPS. There is broad alignment that AI has the potential both to reduce and to amplify existing inequities, depending on how it is implemented.

The committee also recognizes that access to AI tools already varies significantly outside of school environments. As a result, policies that rely primarily on restriction within school may unintentionally widen gaps between students who have access to AI outside of school and those who do not.

There are differing perspectives on how best to address these challenges. Some emphasize limiting AI use to prevent over-reliance and protect foundational learning, while others emphasize the importance of providing equitable access and guided use to ensure all students develop the skills needed to navigate an AI-enabled world.

These perspectives reflect a meaningful tradeoff between protection and opportunity.

The committee recommends that FCCPS adopt an approach to equity that considers both access and outcomes, ensuring that policy supports all students in developing the skills and understanding needed to use AI responsibly.

## Commitments to Consider

### Equity Across the Full Lifecycle of AI Use

The committee recommends that equity considerations apply across all aspects of AI use, including:

access to tools

instructional use

assessment practices

implementation across schools and classrooms

Equity should not be treated as a standalone consideration, but as an ongoing lens applied throughout policy and practice.

### Access and Opportunity

The committee recognizes that unequal access to AI tools outside of school may create unintended advantages for some students.

Policies that restrict AI use within school, without addressing differences in access outside of school, may reinforce or widen these disparities.

The committee recommends that FCCPS consider how to provide equitable access to AI tools and guided experiences within the school environment, so that all students have the opportunity to develop AI literacy and related skills.

### Over-Reliance and Skill Development

The committee recognizes concerns about over-reliance on AI and the potential impact on student learning.

At the same time, the committee emphasizes that limiting access alone does not address over-reliance and may instead shift use to less visible or less supported contexts.

The committee recommends focusing on teaching students how to use AI appropriately, including when not to use it, rather than relying solely on restriction.

### Monitoring and Disparate Impact

The committee recommends that FCCPS monitor the impact of AI use on different student groups.

This may include:

identifying potential algorithmic bias

monitoring for disparate outcomes across student populations

evaluating whether implementation advantages or disadvantages certain groups

There is general agreement that ongoing monitoring is necessary to ensure that AI use does not unintentionally reinforce existing inequities.

## Students with Disabilities and Accessibility

The committee recognizes that AI tools may provide meaningful benefits for students with disabilities, including support for accessibility, communication, and learning.

At the same time, tools that affect protected groups require careful review to ensure they are accessible, appropriate, and free from bias.

The committee recommends that:

accessibility and accommodation needs be considered in all student-facing AI use

tools undergo appropriate review prior to adoption

staff receive guidance and training on appropriate use

There is general agreement that AI should support, but not replace, human decision-making in areas such as IEP and 504 determinations.

## Family and Community Considerations

The committee emphasizes that equity also includes ensuring that families understand how AI is used and how it may affect their children.

Communication should be:

accessible across languages

culturally responsive

clear and practical

Families should be supported in understanding both the opportunities and risks of AI, so they can engage meaningfully in their child’s education.

## Implementation Considerations

The committee recognizes that addressing equity in AI use requires ongoing attention and cannot be fully resolved through policy alone.

Efforts to promote equity should be:

integrated into implementation planning

supported through training and resources

informed by ongoing feedback from students, families, and staff

Policy should provide a clear framework for considering equity, while allowing flexibility to adapt as new challenges and opportunities emerge.
