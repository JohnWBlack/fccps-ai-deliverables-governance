FCCPS AI Advisory Committee Report Outline

Proposed Revised Outline

# 0. Preface

## 0.1 Purpose and Applicability

- This policy establishes FCCPS guidance for the responsible use of artificial intelligence tools in educational and operational contexts.

- It applies to students, staff, contractors, and vendors where relevant to FCCPS functions.

## 0.2 Relationship to Existing FCCPS Policy and Regulation

- This policy should be interpreted alongside existing FCCPS policies and regulations.

- It is intended to extend and clarify those policies where AI changes risk, scale, ambiguity, or implementation burden.

## 0.3 How to Read This Document

- WS-POL provides the main policy shell.

- The other workstreams provide cross-cutting inputs, examples, guardrails, implementation considerations, and areas of agreement or disagreement.

# 1. WS-POL — Policy/Recommendation Drafting

## a. Guiding Principles and Motivation

### Virtues we are trying to capture

- Informed by evidence

- IB-aligned

- Tailored to different grade bands and developmental stages

- Sufficiently flexible and evolving to adapt to changing technology

- Human-centered in design and implementation

- Transparent to students, families, and staff

- Equity-aware from procurement through implementation

### Idealized goals for regulating GenAI in FCCPS

- Articulate general thinking on how allowing GenAI use supports AI literacy and other learning objectives.

- Articulate general thinking on how restricting certain GenAI use protects AI literacy, learning, fairness, and student well-being.

### Challenges this policy must address

- Risks of AI adoption are already visible, while benefits are often context-dependent and uncertain.

- The future development of AI tools is uncertain; capabilities and harms may both increase.

- Policy must protect learning, fairness, trust, and student well-being under conditions of uncertainty.

- Policy should avoid both unchecked adoption and impractical overreaction.

### Decision 1

- FCCPS sees a lesser and less immediate need to accelerate and promote GenAI adoption.

- FCCPS sees a greater and more immediate need to protect learning, fairness, and student well-being by restricting certain GenAI applications where necessary.

### Pragmatic constraints on restriction of GenAI applications

- Limits on the ability to fully enforce GenAI restrictions when work is unproctored.

- Opportunities to move some summative assessments into proctored environments, balanced against practical constraints.

- Importance of preserving trust between students and teachers.

- Need to protect students from patently unfair outcomes, including false accusations of cheating and competing with peers who are in rampant noncompliance with AI restrictions.

### Decision 2

- FCCPS should steer between two extremes: unchecked adoption and impractical overrestriction.

## b. Scope and Triaging

### Decision 3

- Whether the policy should focus primarily on GenAI and related detectors or speak more broadly to AI-enabled systems used in FCCPS contexts.

### Decision 4

- Whether and how the policy should address non-academic AI risks, including mental health, harassment, manipulative interaction, and quasi-social dependence.

### Relationship to existing FCCPS policy

- Identify where existing policies already govern related conduct.

- Identify where AI changes scale, ambiguity, evidentiary conditions, or operational burden enough to require additional guidance.

- Recommend an appendix or crosswalk identifying existing policies that should be reviewed, clarified, or updated.

## c. Definitions

- AI

- Generative AI

- AI detector

- Approved tool

- High-stakes use

- AI assistance

- Disclosure / attribution

- Human decision-maker

- Vendor / data processor / routine use / consent terms as needed

## d. Restrictions on Staff and Teacher Use of AI

### Teacher use in planning and instruction

- Permitted assistive uses

- Conditions on classroom-facing use

- Clear distinction between instructional support and replacement of teacher judgment

### Teacher use in feedback and evaluation

- Decision 5: whether and how teachers may use GenAI in providing feedback and evaluating students

- Distinction between assistive drafting and delegation of evaluative judgment

- Requirement that educators remain responsible for final evaluative feedback and grading

### Use of AI detectors

- Decision 6: whether AI detectors are prohibited, advisory only, or otherwise restricted

- If permitted at all, detectors may not serve as sole evidence and must be subject to human review and due-process safeguards

### Use of GenAI in high-stakes adjudicatory or eligibility contexts

- Decision 7: discipline, IEP/504, and other required determinations remain human-led

- AI may assist but not substitute for qualified personnel

- Use in these contexts must be documented, supervised, and constrained

## e. Student Use

### PreK–5

- Decision 8: teach AI literacy while globally minimizing routine direct student AI use

- Emphasize foundational cognitive, social, and developmental learning before routine reliance on AI

- Restrict anthropomorphic or quasi-social framing of AI as buddy, coach, or human-like authority

- Exercise caution with conversational role-play and historical-figure simulation that may blur the line between human and machine-generated interaction

### Grades 6–12

- Decision 9: establish clearer consensus on use cases and posture by age, subject, and learning goal

- Permit progressively broader categories of use as age, task complexity, and student judgment increase

- Preserve clear expectations that students understand when they are interacting with AI rather than a human

### Categories of anticipated student use

- Prohibited without administrative exception

- Prohibited without teacher allowance

- Allowed unless teacher prohibits

### Interaction and exposure boundaries

- When AI may be used as a task-support tool

- When conversational or role-simulating use requires restriction or approval

- When teacher or administrator approval is required because of developmental, psychological, or instructional concerns

### Citation and Disclosure Expectations

- Decision 10: whether chat logs, process logs, or comparable evidence are required when using GenAI on school assignments

- Disclosure expectations should define what counts as meaningful AI assistance, when disclosure is required, whether logs are required, and which implementation details belong in policy versus guidance

## f. Students with Disabilities and Human Decision Safeguards

- IEP/504 and related determinations remain human-led

- AI may assist but not substitute for qualified personnel

- Tools affecting protected groups require pre-adoption bias and accessibility review

- Ongoing monitoring for disparate impact

- Family notification and staff training where applicable

- Accessibility and accommodation requirements must be considered in any student-facing AI use

## g. Protection of Student Privacy

### Student privacy and data protection

- Data minimization

- Retention and deletion expectations

- Vendor DPA and related privacy requirements

- Notice and consent expectations

- Limits on use of student data beyond routine educational purposes

### Vendor and procurement governance

- Tiered tool approval framework based on risk

- Approval criteria for new AI tools

- Governance for adopting, renewing, monitoring, or discontinuing tools

- Vendor accountability and ongoing review

- Relationship between privacy review and procurement decision-making

## h. Public Accountability Relating to Use of AI by Students and Staff

### Decision 11

- How to scope commitments so that they are implementable

### Commitments to consider

- Public notice

- Consideration of public comment

- Parental opt-out or consent where applicable

### Accountability mechanisms

- Accountable owner(s) for governance and incident response

- Public-facing explanation of AI uses and safeguards at an appropriate level

- Public feedback channel

- Process for review when harms, complaints, or new risks emerge

## i. Equity in Our Community

- Equity and accessibility considerations apply across procurement, student use, implementation, and monitoring.

- This section identifies key equity questions, but equity should not appear only here.

### Over-reliance and inequity

- How can FCCPS prevent over-reliance on GenAI in a way that ameliorates existing inequities and avoids creating new inequities?

### Access and inequity

- How can FCCPS structure access to GenAI tools in a way that ameliorates existing inequities and avoids creating new inequities?

### Monitoring

- Identify algorithmic bias and disparate-impact risks where relevant

- Monitor whether implementation advantages some students, families, or schools over others

## j. School Budgeting and Workload Implications

- AI impacts on teacher workload

- AI impacts on teacher staffing levels

- Implementation burden on teachers and staff

- Capacity requirements for training, support, review, and compliance

## k. Reassessment and Updating of Policy

### Pilot and innovation pathway

- When new AI uses should begin as bounded pilots

- How pilots are evaluated

- Criteria for expansion, pause, or termination

### Measuring effectiveness

- Define anticipated benefits

- Establish baseline measures

- Measure impact over time

- Monitor for unintended effects

### Incident response

- What happens when an AI tool causes harm

- False accusation or integrity harm

- Privacy or data exposure

- Governance and escalation path

### Phased implementation timeline

- What takes effect immediately

- What requires infrastructure, training, or approval processes first

- What should be piloted before full adoption

### Review cadence

- Annual administrative review

- Periodic Board review

- Accelerated review when needed due to incidents, technology change, or outcome data
