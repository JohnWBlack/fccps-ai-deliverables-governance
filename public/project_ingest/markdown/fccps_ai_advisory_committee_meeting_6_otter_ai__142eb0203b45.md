# PIVOT

Here’s how the committee planned to collaborate on finalizing the document, based on this meeting:

## 1. Overall goal and focus

Shift from “everyone writes their own piece” to convergence and condensation:

Move from anthology of individually attributed pieces to integrated, committee‑voice sections.

Focus refinement effort on the policy sections A–O; treat work stream sections as appendix/supporting material rather than heavily condensing those.

## 2. Review and refine prefaces / policy sections

The committee agreed on several key tasks:

Review the existing prefaces for each policy section

Check for:

Neutrality and accuracy of how agreements and disagreements are characterized.

Whether any “disagreements” are real and documented or possibly AI‑inflated summaries (David’s caution at ~[1:04:59–1:05:45]).

If a preface misrepresents positions, members should:

Raise it to you as chair for revision, or

Propose clearer language capturing actual agreement/disagreement.

Identify and reduce redundancy

Look for content repeated across:

Policy sections, and

Work stream sections.

Where something is covered better elsewhere, options include:

Removing a redundant paragraph and adding a short cross‑reference (e.g., “see section M” or “see work stream X”).

For now, redundancy is acceptable if it serves clarity for the board, but the goal is a tighter core policy document.

## 3. Assign “head honchos” to sections

The committee adopted a divide‑and‑conquer model:

For each policy section (A–O), one “head honcho” is responsible for:

Boiling down all contributions in that section into a concise, integrated write‑up.

Incorporating others’ content, not just their own.

Writing in a single committee voice (no “I think”; instead “the committee recommends/believes…”).

These leads were assigned in the meeting (e.g., Tom on Guiding Principles, others on student use, equity, etc.), with the understanding that:

Other members can and should collaborate with the head honcho for that section (send edits, request inclusion of points, etc.).

The head honcho is the integration point, not the sole author of ideas.

## 4. Style and length expectations

You and the committee set informal but clear conventions:

Voice and audience

Address the school board directly.

Use committee voice:

“The committee recommends…”

“The committee believes…”

Avoid first‑person individual voice (“I think…”).

Plain language

Write for lay board members, not specialists.

Prefer clear, non-technical language.

Length targets

For each policy section:

Rough target ½ page to 2 pages, with judgment depending on complexity.

Student use may reasonably run longer, but there is a general incentive to be as short and punchy as possible (Tom’s “Hemingway not Dickens” comment).

Macro target:

A policy document that is digestible (~10–30 pages), not 180+ pages.

Work stream sections remain longer and function as appendix / “how” details rather than core “what” policy.

## 5. Timeline and workflow before the next meeting

The collaboration plan between this meeting and the next:

Asynchronous work (next ~2 weeks)

Each section lead:

Reads all materials in their section.

Produces a condensed, integrated draft (executive‑summary style), drawing from:

Original outline,

Contributions,

Current preface.

Aim to have drafts ready mid‑way (e.g., by Wednesday/Thursday) so:

Other contributors can review,

Authors of original contributions can request key points be kept or adjusted.

Peer feedback loop

Contributors who feel under‑represented:

Contact the section lead directly to:

Ask for inclusion of missing points,

Suggest wording changes,

Flag mischaracterizations.

For purely technical/format errors, people send those directly to you as chair/editor.

## 6. Extended next meeting for live collaboration

The committee decided to use the already scheduled next meeting (April 24) and extend it:

New meeting length: 3 hours (roughly 8:30–11:30), with breaks.

Purpose:

Live co‑editing in a shared Google Doc during a public meeting (as Bethany noted, this is permissible while the meeting is in session).

Condensing and aligning sections:

Review each section’s new integrated draft.

Resolve remaining disagreements where possible.

Make the document structure and navigation clearer (e.g., TOC clean‑up, section merging like F/G/H student use topics).

Format ideas discussed:

Possible breakouts in pairs/small groups to tackle specific clusters, then report back.

Use that time to further tighten language and ensure coherence across sections.

## 7. Handling dissents and minority views

The plan respects that some members strongly disagree on certain issues:

Dissents are allowed and even encouraged when necessary.

Options for dissenters:

Negotiate integration of their concerns into the main section text.

Write a separate dissent (placement TBD—either in a dedicated dissent section or aligned with relevant policy sections).

However, the primary aim is a single, clear main text, not an anthology of 10+ fully separate contributions per topic.

## 8. Interface with the Board

There is a 10‑minute presentation to the School Board on April 28:
