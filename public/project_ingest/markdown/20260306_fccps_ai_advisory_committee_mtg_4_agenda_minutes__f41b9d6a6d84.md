# Summary of Proceedings

## 1) Adoption of the MInutes

The minutes from Meeting #3 were presented for approval. No changes requested were received. The minutes were approved by consent, with one abstention from a member who had not yet reviewed them.

## 2) Adoption of the Agenda

The agenda was presented outlining two primary tasks:

adoption of policy principles based on survey feedback; and

workstream briefings on initial deliverables.

The agenda was adopted by consent.

## 3) Policy Principles Discussion

The committee continued work on defining policy principles that will guide the drafting of AI policy recommendations. These principles were developed from earlier risk and opportunity discussions and refined through two committee surveys.

Members reviewed a set of proposed principles and categorized them into areas of strong agreement (“green zone”), partial alignment (“yellow zone”), and disagreement (“red zone”). The goal remains to identify approximately 8–12 principles to guide policy drafting.

Discussion focused particularly on the concept of a plain-language risk rubric for evaluating AI use cases (e.g., green/yellow/red categories). Members debated the structure and practical implementation of such a rubric. Due to unresolved questions regarding definition and application, the committee agreed to park this item for further consideration.

## 4) Principle on AI Use in Student Feedback

The committee reviewed language related to the use of AI in grading and feedback. Discussion addressed concerns about over-reliance on AI in evaluation, maintaining meaningful teacherstudent relationships, and ensuring transparency.

Several members expressed that fully prohibiting AI use in teacher feedback would be overly restrictive. Others raised concerns about maintaining authentic human engagement in student assessment.

After discussion, the committee agreed to adopt a principle based on the rationale presented, emphasizing that AI use in assessment should preserve meaningful human involvement and transparency while avoiding overly prescriptive restrictions.

## 5) Policy Drafting Workstream

David Berol presented an outline for the structure of the policy recommendation document. The outline included high-level sections such as guiding principles, scope, definitions, staff and student use limitations, privacy protections, accountability, equity considerations, budgeting impacts, and monitoring and reassessment.

The purpose of the outline is to ensure that the policy framework covers all major areas and to guide future drafting work. Members were invited to review and provide feedback to ensure no major policy categories were missing.

## 6) Classroom Experience Workstream

Anubav Vasudevan and Susan Nelson presented preliminary work focused on classroom scenarios involving AI use.

The workstream developed example scenarios illustrating how AI may be used by teachers and students across grade levels. These scenarios are intended to help clarify how policy principles apply in real classroom situations, including edge cases requiring educator discretion.

The group emphasized two key considerations:

AI use should align with sound pedagogical practices grounded in human-centered learning standards, and student safety and privacy must remain central when evaluating AI tools and classroom use.

## 7) Academic Integrity Workstream

David Berol presented an initial framework addressing academic integrity risks related to generative AI.

Key considerations included differences between formative and summative assessments and the challenges of maintaining integrity in unproctored environments. The workstream also highlighted potential disclosure requirements for AI use and emphasized caution regarding AI detection tools.

Members discussed concerns about reliability and fairness in AI detection systems and the importance of avoiding over-reliance on automated detection as evidence of misconduct.

## 8) Equity and Access Workstream

Tom Sabo presented initial work on equity considerations in AI adoption.

The group proposed developing a checklist supported by guiding principles to evaluate AI tools and policies from an equity and accessibility perspective. Members noted that a checklist alone may be insufficient and recommended integrating equity considerations throughout the policy development process.

Discussion also addressed concerns about bias in AI models and the importance of ensuring that AI literacy training includes awareness of fairness and access issues.

## 9) School Board Liaison Report

Bethany Henderson shared updates from the School Board, including feedback from recent AI town hall discussions and additional community input received on the topic.

Members were informed of upcoming town halls, including a community session scheduled for April 18 at the community center. The board encouraged the committee to continue gathering community perspectives and noted that state legislative developments related to technology policy may affect future local policy decisions.

## 10) Next Steps

The committee agreed to shift focus toward policy drafting over the next meetings. Workstreams will continue refining their materials and integrating them into the draft policy structure.

Some policy principles remain unresolved and will be revisited as the drafting process progresses.

## 11) Adjournment

The meeting concluded after the workstream briefings and discussion of next steps.
