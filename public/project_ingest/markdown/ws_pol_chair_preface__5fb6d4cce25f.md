WS-POL-D chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses the foundational terms that make the rest of the policy intelligible and enforceable. The committee broadly agrees that FCCPS needs plain-language, operationally useful definitions for key terms such as AI, generative AI, AI detector, approved tool, high-stakes use, AI assistance, disclosure or attribution, human decision-maker, and vendor or data-processor terms. The committee also broadly agrees that these definitions are not merely editorial; they shape how later sections on student use, staff use, privacy, academic integrity, and accountability will actually function in practice (FCCPS AI Advisory Committee, 2026a, 2026b, 2026c, 2026e). (GitHub)

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how much policy content should be embedded in the definitions themselves. One position favors more neutral, descriptive definitions that clarify technical and operational terms while leaving most normative judgments to later sections. Another favors more interpretive definitions that draw sharper distinctions—for example, between citation and disclosure, constructive and evaluative feedback, or broad fluency and the “when not to use AI” dimension of AI literacy—and that use the definitions section to shape later policy decisions more explicitly. The clearest unresolved definition issue is “AI assistance,” where the committee considered material-contribution, tiered-functional, and inclusive-plus-exempt-list approaches because that term affects disclosure, restriction rules, classroom examples, tool approval, and integrity review (Berol, 2026; FCCPS AI Advisory Committee, 2026b, 2026d).

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The committee agrees that definitions must be plain-language, usable, and aligned with the rest of the policy framework. The remaining disagreement is about how much substantive policy should be carried inside the definitions themselves, especially in terms such as AI assistance, AI literacy, citation, and disclosure (Berol, 2026; FCCPS AI Advisory Committee, 2026d).
