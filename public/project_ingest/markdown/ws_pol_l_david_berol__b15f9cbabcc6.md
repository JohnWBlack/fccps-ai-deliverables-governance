# WS POL L David Berol

WS-POL-L Public Accountability

With respect to any summative assessment of a student or any decision affecting student educational rights (such as a disciplinary record or an IEP), students and parents should have the right to know whether the assessment or decision was made:

(A) without any reliance on AI tools;

(B) with purely non-substantive contributions from AI tools; or

(C) with substantive contributions from an AI tool

The Board should develop policy and regulations spelling out what “substantive” means. With respect to documents bearing a (C) label, students and parents should have the right to inspect the record of substantive AI use contributing to the decision, so that they can discern for themselves how much of the writing and thinking behind that decision was really done by the people who signed off on the document. The Board should develop policy and regulations to clarify the necessary document retention and document request procedures.

The default assumption should be that parents do not want AI tools to be used in substantively planning or drafting summative assessments and critical decision documents affecting their children.  To the extent such AI applications are potentially allowable under FCCPS policy, they should still be contingent on obtaining opt-in from parents.

WS-POL-N Workforce & Budget Implications

AI adoption may have an adverse long-term impact on faculty working conditions and availability of human-based educational services in Falls Church City.  Increasing the scope of teacher responsibilities, concurrent with providing AI tools that purport to make teachers more efficient, risks amounting to “AI replacing teachers,” or at least being perceived as such.

To mitigate these concerns, the Board should reassure the community that it would be inappropriate to cite AI-driven efficiency gains as a justification to:

Reduce the teacher workforce.

Delay the backfill of faculty vacancies.

Increase the scope of teacher responsibilities.
