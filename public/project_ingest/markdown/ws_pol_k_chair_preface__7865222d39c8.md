WS-POL-K chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should protect student privacy, govern AI-related data practices, and establish vendor and procurement controls for AI tools used in educational and operational contexts. The Committee broadly agrees that AI tools should not be treated alike for governance purposes and that review should be stronger when tools involve identifiable student data, sensitive records, system integration, or consequential student decisions. The Committee also broadly agrees that data minimization, retention and deletion expectations, vendor accountability, and some form of tiered review or approval framework are necessary to ensure that AI use remains lawful, responsible, and aligned with community trust.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how broad and how prescriptive the governance framework should be. One position favors a broad, district-wide AI governance model covering AI-enabled tools generally, with formal tiering, stronger procurement controls, and tighter review of vendor practices from adoption through ongoing monitoring. Another favors a narrower focus on generative AI and the most visible student-data risks, emphasizing the need to avoid governance structures that become overly burdensome or difficult to implement in practice. Members also differ on notice, consent, and opt-out expectations: some support stronger family-facing procedural protections, while others caution that expansive commitments may be difficult to operationalize consistently across classrooms and tools.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS needs stronger privacy, data-governance, and vendor-review safeguards for AI than would be appropriate for ordinary low-risk classroom tools. The remaining disagreement is mainly about scope and mechanism: how broad the governance framework should be, how much flexibility to allow for lower-risk uses, and how far policy should go in specifying notice, consent, and procurement requirements directly rather than leaving more detail to guidance and implementation structures.
