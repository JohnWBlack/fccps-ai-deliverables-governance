FCCPS AI Advisory Committee

Classroom Practice and Examples (ver. 1.0)

Below are several grade-banded vignettes describing AI use-cases that illustrate how the values and principles endorsed by this committee, as documented in [V&P doc ref], can be applied in actual classroom settings. Each scenario is followed by a brief description of its potential benefits and risks, and a list of scenario specifications in which the described AI use-case should be allowed, restricted, or prohibited, in accordance with this committee’s values and principles.

These determinations reflect a human-centered approach to AI. The use of AI technologies may be permitted and even encouraged where it supports learning and productivity, but the people involved in administering the use of such technologies must remain accountable to existing standards of sound pedagogical decision-making, evaluative accuracy, and equity and fairness.

The real-world scenarios involving the use of AI that educators will confront in the classroom will bring with them their own set of context-specific challenges. Consequently, the examples described below should be taken as merely illustrative not prescriptive, and we acknowledge that educators must be afforded full allowance to exercise their own discretion on a case-by-case basis.

# 1) Grades K–2 (Student): “Read-Aloud Buddy at Home”

Scenario: A 1st grader uses an AI chatbot and voice assistant at home to generate a story from a prompt (“Tell me a story about…”), interacting with the AI chatbot to practice letter sounds, vocabulary, and spelling (“What letter does the word ‘cake’ end with?”, “What does ‘fragile’ mean?”, “How do you spell ‘digging’?”).

Benefits: Build confidence and engagement through the use of personally curated content.

Risks: Privacy exposure. Generation of inaccurate or age-inappropriate material.

Allowed:

Student employs an AI tool that is supplied by the school and engages with the AI chatbot using only approved teacher-scripted prompts and with adult supervision.

Student’s at-home use of the AI chatbot reinforces in-class instructional programming and aligns with sound pedagogical practices. If, for example, it is determined that excessive use of the chatbot discourages students from engagement with in-print materials, or inhibits creative forms of word-based experimentation (e.g., invented spelling) that are deemed important to achieve early literacy goals, its use should be limited.

Not Allowed:

Student uses the AI-chatbot to generate a story from prompts that reference the names of other students, their photos, or other identifying information.

Student uses a tool that records and collects the child’s voice or personal information without parental approval and FCCPS sanctioned controls.

Student uses a tool that is not supplied by the school or uses that tool without adult supervision.

# 2) Grades 3–5 (Student): “AI Assistance with Images for a Science Poster”

Scenario: A 4th grader is assigned the task of creating a poster for a science class that depicts in visual form the differences between weathering, erosion, and deposition. The relevant information about the topic can be obtained from the various materials that have been officially sanctioned for class use (textbooks and other online sources). The purpose of the assignment is for students to exercise their creativity in order to visually represent concepts presented in class.

Benefits: Supplement irrelevant skills (e.g., artistic ability), which, given the format of an assignment, may have a differential impact on the final product.

Risks: Obviate core skill-building activities. Produce polished work that can misrepresent student knowledge or abilities.

Allowed:

After researching the topic, the student drafts detailed descriptions of the images to be included on the poster and then uses an AI image-generation tool to produce these images and to assist in the layout and design of the poster (“Weathering is kind of like putting rocks in a blender. Draw me a picture of rocks in a blender”, “What would be an effective layout for a poster that includes the attached images?”, “Should the title text be larger in order to be easy-to-read when I present it in front of my class?”). When submitting the poster, the student is required to clearly indicate that an image was generated using AI and to include the specific prompt used to generate that image.

Conditional:

The student uses AI to assist in organizing or summarizing officially sanctioned class materials (“Fetch the definitions of erosion, weathering, and deposition from the textbook”, “Extract all the material from the class sources that relates to weathering, erosion, and deposition and organize in a table”). Especially in earlier grades, when assignments are not just meant to test content knowledge but also to help students acquire core skills deemed essential to later learning, more restrictive limitations on the use of AI may be called for. In this case, part of the purpose of the assignment could be for students to practice navigating larger documents, like a textbook or online resource, in search of specific information. Even if AI systems can reliably perform such preliminary organizational tasks, students should not be encouraged to offload this work to AI systems.

Not Allowed:

Using AI to generate a part or the whole of the poster directly from the assignment prompt (“Draw some pictures illustrating the difference between weathering, erosion, and deposition?”, “Create a poster that illustrates the difference between weathering, erosion, and deposition”)

# 3) Grades 6–8 (Student): “Study Coach for a History Exam”

Scenario: A 7th grader uploads course material to an AI tool and asks it to deliver a concise lecture reviewing the main points covered in the material. The student then asks this AI tool to generate practice questions about the material in the notes in a similar format to the questions that will be included on an upcoming exam. The student asks the AI for feedback assessing the quality of their submitted answers and providing advice as to how they can be improved.

Benefits: Potential to balance inequities created by high-cost instructional aids like extra-curricular classes or private tutors. Provides immediate feedback, tailored to student needs.

Tradeoffs: Overreliance on AI generated answers undermines deep engagement with course content. Possible misinformation and bias in AI generated summaries, questions, and feedback.
