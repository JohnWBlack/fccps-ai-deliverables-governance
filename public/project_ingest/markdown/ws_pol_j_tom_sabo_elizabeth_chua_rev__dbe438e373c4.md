WS-POL-J – Students with Disabilities and Human Decision Safeguards – Tom Sabo and Elizabeth Chua

Protect Students with Disabilities and Multilingual Learners
Ensure AI use does not conflict with IEPs, 504 Plans, or language supports. Legitimate uses such as translation, text-to-speech, or paraphrasing for access should not be broadly prohibited through AI policies or integrity rules.

Rationale: Concerns were raised that blanket bans or rigid integrity rules may unintentionally penalize students who rely on assistive or language supports. Equity requires distinguishing misuse from legitimate access needs. Possible Implications:

Require Special Education and English Learner expertise in AI policy review and/or…

Prohibit AI guidelines that negate approved accommodations without formal review – this could be an overarching rule when adopting AI related policy.

# WS POL-J: Students with Disabilities and Human Decision Safeguards

IEP and 504 Plans must be developed by qualified professionals. Consistent with FERPA, student information or PII may not be put into a generative AI tool.  See WS-DPS for a detailed discussion of tiered clearance levels governing AI use by related service providers — including school psychologists, speech-language pathologists, occupational therapists, and school social workers — at each risk tier.

Staff may use generative AI as a starting point to brainstorm about potential goals and accommodations, but staff cannot rely solely on this output.  All IEP and 504 plans must be reviewed, edited, and customized by human staff. See WS-DPS for a detailed discussion of tiered clearance levels governing AI use by related service providers — including school psychologists, speech-language pathologists, occupational therapists, and school social workers — at each risk tier.

AI and AI tools may be used for accessible materials but must be reviewed by a human before distribution and special attention must be given for biases in AI generated materials.
