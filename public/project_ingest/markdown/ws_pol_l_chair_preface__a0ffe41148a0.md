# WS POL L Chair Preface

WS-POL-L chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should communicate AI-related expectations and safeguards to students, families, staff, and the broader community, and how the district should structure accountability, notice, consent, and feedback processes around AI use. The Committee broadly agrees that successful implementation will require clear, accessible, plain-language communication; identifiable accountability for governance and incident response; and some meaningful way for students, families, and staff to raise concerns or provide feedback when harms, confusion, or new risks emerge. The Committee also broadly agrees that public-accountability commitments should be real and understandable, but also scoped in ways that FCCPS can implement consistently over time.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how far policy should go in creating formal notice, consent, and opt-out expectations. One position favors stronger family-facing procedural protections, including clearer notice of substantive AI use in sensitive contexts and, in some cases, stronger consent or inspection rights. Another favors a more bounded framework centered on advance notice, approved-tool governance, data minimization, accountable owners, and feedback channels, while cautioning that overly broad notice, registry, or opt-out commitments may become difficult to operationalize consistently across tools and classrooms. Members also differ on whether public accountability should be framed primarily as family-facing rights and transparency, or more as internal governance with public explanation and review.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should communicate AI use and safeguards more clearly, provide accountable points of ownership, and create a process for feedback and review when concerns arise. The remaining disagreement is mainly about how strong and expansive notice, consent, and opt-out commitments should be, and how much of public accountability should be expressed as direct procedural rights versus governance structures supported by transparent communication.
