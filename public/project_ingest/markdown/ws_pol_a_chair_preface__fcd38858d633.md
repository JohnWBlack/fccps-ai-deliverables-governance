# WS POL A Chair Preface

Below is draft language in two parts: first, the chair’s preface language for WS-POL-A to hold until all contributions are in; second, your own substantive draft for WS-POL-A reflecting a design-first, guardrailed-integration viewpoint.

1. Preface language for WS-POL-A (hold for later)

Neutral narrative paragraph

The Committee’s discussion of guiding principles and motivation reflects substantial agreement on core values, even where members differ on policy posture. Across meetings and draft materials, members repeatedly emphasized that any FCCPS AI policy should be evidence-informed, aligned with student learning and FCCPS educational goals, tailored to developmental stages, human-centered in implementation, transparent to students, families, and staff, attentive to equity, and adaptable as technology changes. The Committee also broadly agreed that policy must protect student learning, assessment integrity, fairness, trust, privacy, and student well-being under conditions of uncertainty, while avoiding both unchecked adoption and impractical overrestriction.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members expressed differing views about default posture. One position places greater weight on restrictions, especially where AI may weaken learning, distort assessment, or create fairness risks that schools cannot reliably manage. Another position places greater weight on thoughtful, well-governed integration, arguing that students and educators need structured opportunities to build AI literacy and applied competence in a society increasingly shaped by AI. Members also differed on how much policy should rely on restriction, design choices, proctoring, disclosure, human review, or pilot-based governance to manage risk. These differences are meaningful and should be made visible to the Board rather than harmonized away.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees on the need for strong guardrails and human accountability, but not on every default rule for when AI use should be limited, permitted, or affirmatively encouraged. Where disagreement remains, the Board should understand that these are not disagreements over whether student learning and well-being matter, but over how best to protect those interests while preparing students and staff for an AI-shaped future.
