WS-POL-H chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should translate broad student-use principles into workable classroom categories, controls, and interaction boundaries. The Committee broadly agrees that not all student AI use is the same and that policy should distinguish among different types of use in order to improve clarity, consistency, and enforceability across classrooms. The Committee also broadly agrees that categories should be applied in light of grade band, learning goal, subject, and assignment context, and that controls should be credible enough to support real implementation rather than rely mainly on after-the-fact suspicion or weak enforcement.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how prescriptive the category framework should be and how much discretion should remain with teachers. One position favors clearer district-level categories, stronger prohibitions for uses that undermine independent reasoning or assessment validity, and control strategies built into assignment design and controlled follow-on assessments. Another position also supports a common taxonomy, but places greater weight on teacher judgment, contextual flexibility, and a larger space for conditional use where AI may support learning if bounded appropriately. Members also differ on how explicitly policy should regulate interaction styles such as task-support, task-substitution, conversational use, and role-simulating use, especially where developmental, psychological, or instructional concerns may arise.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should adopt a shared framework for categorizing student AI use and for defining when additional controls or approvals are required. The remaining disagreement is mainly about how tightly those categories should be standardized, how much discretion teachers should retain in applying them, and how far policy should go in regulating interaction boundaries as distinct from output or product alone.
