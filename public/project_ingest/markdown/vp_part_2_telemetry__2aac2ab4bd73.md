# N/X follow-up themes (aggregated, paraphrased)

## Definition locks

D1 (high-stakes definition): ambiguity/objection to the concept (“high-stakes/summative” as a workable axis); concern that “stakes-based restriction” can be internally inconsistent without a clearer risk model.

D2 (AI assistance scope): concern that “AI assistance” must cover more than writing generation; request for benchmarking against other districts / a clearer basis for categories.

D3 (risk rubric): support for rubric direction, but desire to define risk as probability × impact and enumerate risks; concern that “permitted/conditional/restricted” may collapse into “conditional” unless operationalized.

## Decision-ready rewrites

R1 (use-case guidance clause): options felt too similar / too “thin”; desire for stronger operational commitment (ownership, update triggers, usable formats).

R2 (assessment validity posture): “design/process evidence” and “restrictions” are not opposites—design implements restrictions; objection to limiting rigor only to “high-stakes.”

R3 (detectors): strongest concern is informal or teacher-run detector use creating bias/priming even if “non-dispositive”; preference for strict constraints or prohibition outside district-controlled validation.

R4 (notice/consent/opt-out): need to define scope of “student data”; concern that “where feasible” implies mandatory AI; skepticism about operational burden of advance notice/consent if not tightly scoped.

R5 (human-centered rule): desire for an operational rule tied to learning goals (e.g., require non-AI evidence when goals require it); avoid vague “balance”; interest in piloting AI where benefits exceed what offline/hands-on experiences reliably provide.

R6 (teacher-owned feedback): main concern is clarity/logic around what counts as “evaluative” vs “formative,” plus workload/content-overflow risk if AI increases volume without improving signal.
