# WS-POL-O — Reassessment and Updating of Policy

## Decision — Committee Perspective (No Full Consensus)

The committee generally agrees that any AI policy must be adaptive and regularly updated in response to evolving technology, emerging risks, and observed outcomes in practice. There is broad alignment that static policy will quickly become outdated.

The committee also recognizes that AI capabilities are evolving rapidly and that students are already interacting with these tools outside of school. As a result, policy reassessment should not only respond to risks, but also ensure that FCCPS continues to prepare students to engage with AI tools thoughtfully and responsibly.

There are important tradeoffs regarding how reassessment should occur. These include balancing the need for timely updates with the risk of constant change creating confusion, determining the appropriate level of formal review versus administrative adjustment, and ensuring that updates are informed by real-world data rather than reactive responses.

The committee recommends a structured but flexible approach to policy reassessment that supports continuous learning while maintaining stability and clarity.

## Commitments to Consider

### Pilot and Innovation Pathway

The committee recommends that new or higher-risk uses of AI begin as bounded pilots rather than immediate system-wide implementation.

Pilots should:

have clearly defined goals and intended outcomes

include guardrails appropriate to the level of risk

be limited in scope and duration

Piloting should also be used as an opportunity to better understand how AI can support learning, not only to identify risks.

### Measuring Effectiveness

The committee recommends that FCCPS define clear criteria for evaluating the impact of AI use.

This may include:

identifying anticipated benefits (e.g., instructional support, efficiency, access)

establishing baseline conditions prior to implementation

measuring outcomes over time

Evaluation should consider both benefits and unintended effects, including impacts on student learning, equity, and teacher workload.

### Incident Response

The committee agrees that FCCPS should have a clear process for responding to incidents involving AI tools.

Examples of incidents may include:

false accusations related to academic integrity

privacy or data exposure concerns

harmful or inappropriate outputs affecting students

Response processes should include:

clear escalation pathways

defined responsibility for review and action

timely communication where appropriate

Incident response should prioritize student well-being, fairness, and transparency.

## Governance and Review Structures

### Phased Implementation

The committee recommends that implementation occur in phases, recognizing that some elements can be implemented immediately while others require additional infrastructure, training, or evaluation.

This may include:

identifying which practices can begin immediately

determining which require pilot programs

sequencing more complex implementations over time

A phased approach supports thoughtful adoption and reduces disruption.

### Review Cadence

The committee recommends establishing a regular review cycle for AI policy and related guidance.

This may include:

annual administrative review

periodic School Board review

accelerated review when triggered by significant incidents, technological change, or new evidence

Review processes should be predictable, but also responsive when needed.

## Feedback and Continuous Improvement

The committee emphasizes that reassessment should be informed by ongoing input from students, families, and staff.

Feedback mechanisms described in WS-POL-H should be integrated into the reassessment process, ensuring that lived experience informs policy updates.

Policy should evolve based on:

observed outcomes in classrooms

implementation challenges

emerging risks and opportunities

## Implementation Considerations

The committee recognizes that continuous reassessment must be balanced with the need for stability and clarity.

Frequent or poorly communicated changes may create confusion for educators and families, while delayed updates may limit the ability to respond to new risks or opportunities.

Policy should therefore define a clear framework for reassessment while allowing flexibility for timely and practical updates.
