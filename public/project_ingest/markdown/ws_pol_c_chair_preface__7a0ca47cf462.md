# WS POL C Chair Preface

WS-POL-C Chair Preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how broadly FCCPS policy should define the AI problem it is trying to govern, how AI-related uses and risks should be triaged, and how new AI guidance should relate to existing FCCPS policy and regulation. The Committee broadly agrees that AI policy should be interpreted alongside existing FCCPS policies and should extend or clarify them where AI changes scale, ambiguity, evidentiary conditions, or implementation burden. The Committee also broadly agrees that some form of triaging is necessary, whether through risk categories, use-case distinctions, or supporting guidance, so that policy can remain both principled and practical.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how broad the policy scope should be. One position favors a narrower focus on generative AI, detectors, academic integrity, and the most immediate classroom-facing issues. Another favors a broader scope that includes AI-enabled systems used in FCCPS contexts more generally, along with privacy, procurement, wellbeing, equity, and governance concerns that may not be reducible to GenAI alone. Members also differ on whether non-academic risks such as manipulative interaction, harassment, and quasi-social dependence should be addressed directly in Board-facing policy language or handled more fully through supporting guidance, implementation frameworks, or existing policies.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS needs a clear way to define what is in scope, what risks require differentiated treatment, and where AI-specific guidance is needed beyond existing policy. The remaining disagreement is mainly about breadth and placement: how much should be stated directly in the AI policy itself, and how much should be handled through crosswalks, guidance, and supporting governance structures.
