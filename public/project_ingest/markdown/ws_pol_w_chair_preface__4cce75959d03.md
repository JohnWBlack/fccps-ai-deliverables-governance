WS-POL-E Chair Preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should govern staff and teacher use of AI in planning, instruction, feedback, evaluation, integrity-related review, and other consequential school functions. The Committee broadly agrees that AI may play a bounded assistive role for educators, especially in planning, operational support, translation, and preparation of instructional materials, but that such use must augment rather than replace professional judgment, human accountability, or the educator’s responsibility for student-facing decisions. The Committee also broadly agrees that evaluative judgments, grading, discipline-related determinations, and other high-stakes decisions must remain human-led, and that policy should remain clear and communicable without collapsing into overly detailed implementation rules.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ most sharply on teacher use of AI in feedback and evaluation. One position holds that evaluative feedback should be the teacher’s authentic work product and that even AI-assisted drafting in this area risks undermining trust, authenticity, and the student-teacher relationship unless subjected to unusually strong disclosure, consent, and review requirements; some members would prohibit such use altogether except under very narrow conditions. Another position permits limited assistive use, especially where AI helps with planning, accessibility, translation, or drafting support, so long as the educator meaningfully reviews, personalizes, and fully owns the final student-facing output. Members also differ on AI detectors: there is broad concern about reliability and fairness, but while some members would allow at most highly constrained advisory use with human review and due-process protections, others recommend a provisional or complete bar because detector use is seen as too likely to produce false accusations, confirmation bias, and unfair treatment.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that teacher judgment must remain primary, that student-facing evaluative decisions must remain human-led, and that any educator use of AI should be governed in ways that preserve trust, clarity, and accountability. The remaining disagreement is mainly about how far assistive use may extend into student feedback and evaluative workflows, and whether detector tools have any legitimate role beyond, at most, highly constrained non-dispositive review.
