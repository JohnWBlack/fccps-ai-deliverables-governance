WS-POL-E Chair Preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should govern staff and teacher use of AI across planning, instruction, feedback, evaluation, detection, and other consequential school functions. The Committee broadly agrees that some assistive uses of AI by educators may be appropriate, especially in planning and instructional preparation, but that AI must not replace teacher judgment or weaken human accountability. The Committee also broadly agrees that AI outputs should not serve as the sole basis for grades, discipline, placement, intervention, or academic-integrity determinations, and that uses involving identifiable student data or high-stakes decisions require stronger safeguards, approval processes, and human oversight.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ most sharply on teacher use of AI in feedback and evaluation. One position holds that even AI-assisted drafting of student feedback or preliminary evaluation risks undermining trust, teacher credibility, and the student-teacher relationship, and should therefore be prohibited. Another position allows for limited assistive drafting under strict conditions, provided that the educator meaningfully reviews, personalizes, and fully owns the final feedback and judgment. Members also differ on detector tools: some support limited advisory use with strong safeguards, while others recommend avoiding them entirely in integrity processes because of reliability, fairness, and due-process concerns.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that teacher judgment must remain primary and that high-stakes school decisions must remain human-led. The remaining disagreement is mainly about whether AI may ever assist in preparing student feedback or evaluative materials, and whether detector tools can play any legitimate role beyond highly constrained, non-dispositive advisory use.
