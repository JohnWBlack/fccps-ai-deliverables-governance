2. Purpose of Meeting

Review the integrated draft package and assess what is ready for Board use versus what still requires refinement.

Identify where broad alignment exists, where differences remain live, and what should be incorporated, preserved as alternatives, or carried forward as explicit dissent.

Establish a converge-and-condense workflow for producing a shorter, more board-readable advisory package before the next checkpoint.

3. Key Discussion Areas

3.1 Integrated Draft Package Review

John Black reported that the committee now has a complete but not yet board-ready package spanning policy sections and supporting workstream materials.

Members discussed the current structure: WS-POL sections as the core policy/recommendation document and workstream deliverables as supporting material.

Committee noted that some redundancy is intentional, especially in cross-cutting areas such as equity, implementation, and communications, but agreed the document must become easier to navigate and digest.

Takeaway:

Committee has moved from content collection into refinement and synthesis of a near-final advisory package.

3.2 Accuracy, Neutrality, and Treatment of Differences

David Berol urged close review of section prefaces to ensure they do not overstate disagreement, create false dichotomies, or mischaracterize the breadth of committee views.

John Black agreed that section prefaces should be tightened where needed and noted that prior feedback had already led to stronger sourcing and narrower, more defensible claims.

Members agreed that disagreements should be preserved when real, but that wording differences or weakly supported distinctions should not be presented as substantive conflict.

Takeaway:

Committee will review prefaces for neutrality and accuracy before further integration.

3.3 Document Structure, Redundancy, and Appendix Treatment

Members discussed whether policy sections should remain multi-contribution anthologies or be converted into single integrated sections.

Thomas Colvin, Elizabeth Chua, Jillian Burkley, and Jerrod Anderson supported a shorter executive-summary approach that synthesizes contributions rather than preserving each one as a separately attributed section in the main text.

Committee generally aligned on treating workstream sections as appendix/supporting material and focusing condensation effort on the WS-POL sections.

Takeaway:

Main policy package should be condensed into integrated sections, with workstream materials retained primarily as supporting appendices.

3.4 Converge-and-Condense Workflow

Committee agreed to shift from an anthology of individually attributed contributions to integrated sections written in a single committee voice.

Members discussed using the existing policy sections as the organizing skeleton, with one person responsible for boiling each section down and collaborators providing edits or missing points directly to that section lead.

Jerrod Anderson emphasized that the Board will benefit most from concise, plain-language summaries that clearly surface major agreements and remaining tensions without overwhelming detail.

Takeaway:

Committee entered a convergence phase centered on integrated section summaries rather than continued accumulation of standalone submissions.

3.5 Section Ownership (“Head Honchos”) and Drafting Assignments

The committee assigned a single “head honcho” to each WS-POL section to produce an integrated summary in committee voice.

Assignments confirmed during and after the meeting included: Thomas Colvin (A and L), John Black (B), Adriana Palacios (C and O), Jillian Burkley (D and K), Susan Nelson (E), Elizabeth Chua (consolidated F + G), Anubav Vasudevan (consolidated H + I), Thomas Sabo (J and M), and David Berol (N).

Members retained discretion to collaborate with section leads, propose insertions, and flag omissions or mischaracterizations directly to the relevant lead and/or the Chair.

Takeaway:

Each WS-POL section now has a designated integration lead responsible for condensing and synthesizing committee input.

3.6 Style, Length, and Board Readability

Committee agreed that final WS-POL sections should generally aim for one-half to two pages, with longer allowances for the consolidated student-use sections.

Members emphasized plain language, committee voice, and brevity, with Thomas Colvin and Jerrod Anderson stressing that the document should be short enough to be read and acted upon by Board members.

Cross-references may be used where helpful, but members generally preferred clarity over aggressive de-duplication at this stage.

Takeaway:

Final drafting standard is concise, board-readable prose that preserves substance without unnecessary length.

3.7 Extended Working Session and Near-Term Timeline

Committee discussed the need for a longer working session to support live editing and alignment.

Members agreed to extend the already scheduled April 24 meeting to approximately three hours, with flexibility for collaborative editing and possible breakouts during the public meeting.

The Chair set an interim expectation that integrated section drafts be submitted by April 15, allowing time for review and incorporation before the April 24 working session.

Takeaway:

Committee adopted an accelerated integration schedule anchored by section drafts, peer feedback, and an extended April 24 working session.

3.8 Board Interface and Presentation Planning

Jerrod Anderson reminded the committee of the upcoming April 28 Board presentation and encouraged advance sharing of a shorter, more digestible package rather than waiting for final polish.

John Black confirmed that he would prepare a 10-minute overview for the Board covering the committee’s work, major areas of agreement, and key unresolved differences, and would circulate materials to the committee in advance.

Takeaway:

Committee linked document condensation directly to the upcoming Board-facing presentation and review sequence.
