# WS POL O Chair Preface

WS-POL-O chair preface

Neutral narrative paragraph

This section addresses how FCCPS should reassess, update, and govern AI policy over time as technology, educational uses, and associated risks continue to change. The Committee broadly agrees that AI policy should not be treated as static and that FCCPS needs a structured way to review new uses, measure effects, respond to harms, and revise course when needed. The Committee also broadly agrees that policy should distinguish between what may take effect immediately and what should depend on infrastructure, training, approval processes, or bounded testing before broader adoption.

Range of positions paragraph

Within that shared foundation, members differ on how formal and prescriptive the updating framework should be. One position favors a clearer pilot-first model with defined success measures, monitoring, and explicit criteria for expansion, pause, or termination before new uses are scaled. Another favors a more flexible continuous-improvement approach, cautioning that terms such as pilot, expansion, and new use can become vague or overly bureaucratic if they are not carefully defined. Members may also differ on how specific policy should be about incident triggers, rollback criteria, phased implementation, and the relative role of administrative review versus periodic School Board review.

Short dissent-friendly formulation

The Committee agrees that FCCPS should revisit AI policy regularly and should have a clear way to evaluate benefits, monitor harms, and adjust course as conditions change. The remaining disagreement is mainly about mechanism and specificity: whether policy should rely more on bounded pilots and explicit off-ramps, or on broader continuous review and iterative adjustment over time.
