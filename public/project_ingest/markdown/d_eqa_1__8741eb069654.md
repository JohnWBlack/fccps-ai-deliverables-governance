Equity and Access

Checklists offer a practical tool, but are unlikely to be comprehensive, especially in the face of rapidly developing technology.  Therefore, it is important to offer multiple tools that can be used to ensure that FCCPS makes decisions that promote equity and are accessible.

Starting Guiding Principles:

FCCPS will evaluate AI tools and AI usage for equity and accessibility from the procurement stage to the classroom, including  administrative uses.  FCCPS will monitor and evaluate AI usage in terms of equity and access, and collect data on reported bias incidences.  Monitoring and evaluation should be incorporated into relevant existing school structures. The committee must include, but is not limited to, School Technology Professionals, Special Education and English Language Learner coordinators.

Topics related to equity will be included as part of AI Literacy for students and staff.  This will include understanding how models are trained and how to identify algorithmic bias.

3) DEEP-DIVE PROMPTS (COMPLETE ONLY FOR ⚠️/⛔ ITEMS in each section)

3A) Access & opportunity gaps 
• What minimum access is required (time, device, bandwidth, accounts)? 
• What is the equitable alternative (offline option, school-provided access window, district tool, don’t use AI)? 
• What guidance ensures students aren’t penalized for access limitations?

3B) Disability & accommodations 
• Which accommodations apply (TTS/STT, extended time, reduced distraction, etc.)? 
• How does this undermine approved supports? 
• What staff guidance/PD is required for consistent implementation?

3C) Language supports 
• Are translation/paraphrase supports well allowed, conditional, or prohibited by task type?

• Are there alternatives which apply that have with good multilingual supports?
• How do we preserve learning goals while supporting access?

3D) Integrity & enforcement fairness 
• What evidence in addition to detectors will staff use (process evidence, drafts, conferencing, reflections)? 
• What evidence or compilation of evidence is explicitly prohibited (detector-only decisions)? 
• What is an equitable escalation path for disputes?

3E) Safety / Wellbeing supports

• What are the methods of accessing harmful content or risky contact?

• Are there non-risky alternatives or is this an inherent risk of leveraging AI?

3F) Privacy/Data/Records

• Vendors data use policy and practice must be vetted

• AI platforms should be transparent about how data is used; opt-out allowed

3G) Staff capacity

• Is it reasonable to expect for teachers to leverage the proposed tools/workflow/capability with minimal training or overhead for mandatory capabilites?

• Tiers? Priorities? Optional but not required training, but required training if they are going to use the tools?

3H) Vendor Tool/POC checks

• How did the AI vendor train its models? What data? Was it multilingual?

• Does the AI vendor rely on autotranslation to handle multilingual use cases? If so, how accurate is the autotranslation?
• What bias testing did the vendor conduct? How can the vendor be sure that the models are not biased?

What AI alignment tools were used in product development and how extensive were they leveraged? Does that AI alignment actually work?

Purpose (2 sentences): WS-EQA produced guiding principles and an Equity Impact Checklist to prevent unintended harm and monitor disparities. The checklist alone seemed insufficient for providing guidance on intent, especially in the face of rapidly developing technology and growing use cases.

We do not think that AI guiding principles should be left out as we are not the ones applying these checklists on the day-day. If we don’t really understand what/how these checklists could be used then it’s not going to be useful. Checklists won’t cover every scenario that would come up. We believe it’s more useful to outline something that has our intent behind it.

Top 3 equity risks to add to risk register (3 bullets): 
• _The checklist alone is not sufficient. We need an equitable AI literacy program in place AKA we need to consider equitability in the context of AI literacy training. Is everyone getting the training that they need (varying needs on training).  
• _Using AI technology is inherently risky and prone to bias because of the training data and opacity of the system to most users; feasible options for use cases driven by smaller language models or agentic framework for particular applications should be considered. ____ 
• _The other AI implementations need to be driven in the context of this checklist. It would be insufficient to implement a checklist after we already have other decisions in place to leverage going forward. For instance, we need to develop an AI literacy program while considering elements used in this checklist.

• Too often equity and access is tacked on as an afterthought in policies.  When this happens, practices get set early that can lead to inequities and are harder to fix.  The policy should consider equity access from the start - from procurement to practice.
