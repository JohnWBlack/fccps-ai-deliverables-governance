The goal of the Communications and Community Input plan is to guide discussion around how and when FCCPS will introduce artificial intelligence in a manner that prioritizes student safety, academic integrity, transparency, and responsible innovation while ensuring community input informs policy decisions.

Purpose
This plan ensures artificial intelligence is introduced into classrooms with community understanding, clear safety guardrails, and ongoing accountability, rather than through unstructured or unexamined adoption with feedback and stakeholder engagement as a primary part of the plan not an afterthought.

The engagement strategy ensures FCCPS AI policy is:

Transparent

Protective of children

Educationally responsible

Informed by parent, educator, and community input

Engagement Methods 
Effective community engagement requires multiple methods tailored to different audiences because stakeholders interact with schools—and technology—in very different ways. Parents may prefer town halls or written feedback opportunities, educators often respond best to structured surveys and listening sessions, and students provide valuable insight through advisory groups where they feel comfortable sharing their perspectives. Using a variety of engagement approaches improves perception and trust, signaling that the district is not making decisions in isolation but is actively listening to the community it serves. It also strengthens buy-in, because stakeholders are more likely to support policies they had an opportunity to understand and shape. For complex issues involving technology, student safety, academic integrity, and classroom practices, the perspectives of many groups must be considered early. When engagement is broad and structured, it helps surface concerns, identify unintended risks, and clarify misconceptions before policies are finalized. In contrast, relying on a single feedback channel can leave important voices unheard and allow misunderstandings

or concerns to grow unchecked. A diverse engagement strategy therefore acts as both a trust-building mechanism and a risk-management tool, ensuring the district gathers balanced input and prevents issues from escalating or “snowballing” as new technologies are introduced into schools.

Message Pillars
Clear, balanced, and consistent messaging will be essential to successfully introduce and govern AI use in schools. Because artificial intelligence touches on sensitive areas such as student safety, academic integrity, technology access, and classroom practices, stakeholders will naturally have questions and concerns. Establishing a set of defined message pillars ensures the district communicates with clarity, transparency, and alignment across all channels, whether information is coming from school leaders, teachers, board members, or district communications. Consistency helps prevent confusion, misinformation, or mixed signals, while repetition reinforces understanding over time as the community becomes more familiar with the topic. When the same core messages are delivered repeatedly—across town halls, staff discussions, student forums, and written communications—it builds confidence that the district is approaching AI thoughtfully and responsibly. A disciplined message track therefore supports shared understanding, trust, and informed participation, ensuring stakeholders clearly understand both the opportunities and the safeguards guiding AI policy in the distri

ct.

FCCPS K–12 AI policy messaging must be guided by rules with contextual flexibility to accomplish five objectives:

Demystify AI

Address safety and risk head-on

Clarify guardrails

Empower responsible use

Create feedback loops

There are 5 Pillars to guide message creation which will flow to assets such as a website, fact sheets, flyers, talking points, etc.

Pillar 1 — Safety and Protection First

The district’s first responsibility is protecting students.

Key messages:

AI access must be age-appropriate and supervised.

Safeguards will prevent exposure to harmful or inappropriate content.

Student privacy and data security will be protected.

Core idea: Innovation will never come before student safety.

Pillar 2 — Academic Integrity and Learning

AI must support learning, not replace it.

Key messages:

Students must demonstrate original thinking.

AI-assisted work must be appropriately attributed.

AI cannot replace foundational learning skills.

Core idea: AI should enhance learning—not undermine it.

Pillar 3 — Clear Guardrails for Responsible Use

Students and educators need clear expectations.

Key messages:

Grade-band expectations will define appropriate AI use.

Approved tools will be identified by the district.

Teachers will guide appropriate classroom use.

Core idea: Clear rules help students use powerful tools responsibly.

Pillar 4 — Equity and Access

AI should not widen learning gaps.

Key messages:

All students should have fair access to learning tools.

AI literacy will be integrated into digital citizenship education.

Schools will ensure equitable opportunities for skill development.

Core idea: Responsible AI should expand opportunity, not create advantage for only some students.

Pillar 5 — Transparency and Community Partnership

AI policy will be shaped with community input especially educators and parents.

Key messages:

Families will be informed before major classroom changes.

Community feedback will be actively reviewed.

Policy updates will be shared publicly.

Core idea: AI governance must be transparent and accountable.

Feedback Capture Plan

To ensure community input is organized, transparent, and actionable, we will use a structured feedback management process.  This process allows the AI Committee and School Board to identify meaningful trends while mapping to considerations that need to be incorporated into the policy.  FCCPS will establish structured listening channels to ensure community concerns and insights inform AI policy development as phase one and will use outreach and communications to educate stakeholders on an ongoing basis in phase two.

Centralized Feedback Intake

While one-off emails will be accepted, the preference and recommendation is for all written community feedback will flow through one official intake channel. Such as a dedicated email address or a structured online form to collect submissions stakeholders. Centralizing submissions prevents fragmented communication and allows the committee to analyze feedback systematically.

This ensures feedback is:

Consistent

Documented

Trackable

Accessible for review

Recommendation is a form for consistency using AirOps.  Example:

Community Survey

Teachers and staff

Topics will include:

Privacy and data protection

Grade-band expectations for AI use

Survey findings will be published in summary form.

Feedback from Emails, Surveys and Listening Tours (regardless of format) will be catalog and tagged in Airops. Example:

Tracking and Cataloging System

All submissions will be logged in a centralized tracking system.

Each submission will be cataloged with fields such as:

School or grade band (if applicable)

Topic area

Summary of submission

Status or follow-up actions

This system ensures the district can monitor volume, themes, and response needs over time.

Coding Framework for Feedback Analysis

To identify meaningful trends, all feedback will be coded using a consistent topic taxonomy.

The committee will establish a predefined set of categories before reviewing submissions.

Example topic areas may include:

Student safety and content exposure

Academic integrity

Privacy and student data protection

Classroom use guidelines

Equity and access

Instructional benefits

Technology concerns

Coding allows feedback to be analyzed objectively rather than treated as isolated comments.

Trend Identification

Once submissions are coded, the committee/School Board will review trends on a monthly or quarterly basis.

Trend analysis may include:

Most common concerns raised

Emerging questions from families

Areas of strong support or consensus

New risks or implementation challenges

Trend summaries help the committee understand:

Where additional guidance may be needed

Where communication should be improved

How policy adjustments may be warranted

Transparency Dashboard

To maintain public trust, FCCPS will publish periodic updates summarizing:

Community feedback received; total submissions received

Feedback by stakeholder group; top themes identified

Policy adjustments made

Approved AI tools and use cases

Safety safeguards implemented

This ensures the community understands how their input shapes FCCPS policy.

Draft 90-Day Community Rollout Timeline

Introducing artificial intelligence into a school environment requires a deliberate and transparent approach that allows time for understanding, discussion, and thoughtful policy development. The 90-Day Community Rollout Timeline provides a structured pathway for the district to move from early awareness to informed decision-making while actively involving the community along the way. Its goal is to ensure that educators, families, students, and community partners have clear opportunities to learn about AI, share perspectives, and help shape responsible guardrails before any broad implementation occurs. By pacing engagement over several phases—preparation, community listening, and policy development—the timeline helps the district build trust, gather meaningful feedback, and reduce uncertainty around a complex and rapidly evolving technology. This intentional process supports sound governance while ensuring that student safety, academic integrity, and community confidence remain at the center of the district’s AI strategy.
