# WS-POL-e — Student Use

## Decision — Committee Perspective (No Full Consensus)

The committee generally agrees that student use of AI should be developmentally appropriate and aligned with learning objectives. There is broad agreement that AI use must support, not replace, student learning, and that expectations should be clear and consistent.

The committee also recognizes that AI tools are already widely accessible outside of school and are increasingly part of students’ everyday academic and social environments. As a result, policies that rely primarily on restriction may be difficult to enforce consistently and may unintentionally encourage non-transparent use.

There are differing perspectives on the appropriate balance between restricting AI use to protect learning and enabling guided use to build AI literacy and responsible habits. These perspectives reflect a meaningful tradeoff between risk mitigation and student preparation.

The committee recommends that FCCPS adopt a balanced approach that prioritizes student learning while also preparing students to use AI tools thoughtfully, transparently, and responsibly.

## Commitments to Consider

### Grade-Banded Approach

The committee supports a developmentally appropriate progression of AI use across grade levels.

This progression may include:

Early grades: primarily educator-directed exposure, with limited direct student interaction

Middle grades: guided use with structured expectations and emphasis on understanding AI capabilities and limitations

Upper grades: increased autonomy with clear expectations for appropriate use, disclosure, and evaluation

There is general agreement that exposure should increase with age and maturity, but that this exposure should be intentional and guided, not left to informal or unsupported use.

### Guided Use and AI Literacy

The committee recommends that student interaction with AI be structured in ways that support learning and skill development.

This includes:

helping students understand how AI works and where it may be unreliable

teaching students how to use AI as a tool to support thinking, not replace it

reinforcing critical thinking, originality, and academic integrity

The committee emphasizes that avoiding or delaying exposure entirely may limit students’ ability to develop these skills in a meaningful way.

### Categories of Student Use

The committee supports defining clear categories of student use, such as:

prohibited without administrative exception

prohibited without teacher allowance

allowed unless specifically restricted

Clear categorization can help reduce confusion and support consistent expectations across classrooms, while still allowing flexibility based on instructional goals.

### Interaction and Exposure Boundaries

The committee recommends that FCCPS define appropriate boundaries for how students interact with AI tools.

This may include:

when AI may be used as a task-support tool

when conversational or role-simulating use requires restriction or supervision

when teacher or administrator approval is required

There is general agreement that younger students may require more structured boundaries, while older students can engage with greater autonomy under clear expectations.

## Citation and Disclosure Expectations

The committee generally agrees that transparency around AI use is important to maintaining academic integrity and trust.

Students should be expected to disclose meaningful AI assistance in their work, particularly when it contributes to the development of ideas, structure, or content.

There is no full consensus on requiring submission of AI interaction logs. Key tradeoffs include:

transparency versus student privacy

practicality and burden on teachers

the risk of over-standardizing classroom practices

The committee recommends defining clear expectations for disclosure while allowing flexibility in implementation.

## Student Experience and Clarity

The committee emphasizes that expectations must be clearly communicated to students.

Students should understand:

when AI use is permitted

when it is conditionally allowed

when it is restricted

Inconsistent expectations across classrooms may create confusion and undermine both learning and accountability. FCCPS should support alignment while allowing appropriate flexibility for educators.

## Equity Considerations in Student Use

The committee recognizes that access to AI tools outside of school is uneven and may create unintended advantages for some students.

Policies that rely heavily on restriction may exacerbate these differences by limiting access within school while access continues outside of it.

The committee recommends that FCCPS consider how student use policies can support equitable access and responsible use, rather than focusing solely on limitation.

## Implementation Considerations

The committee recognizes that expectations for student AI use must be practical and enforceable.

Overly rigid or complex rules may be difficult to implement consistently and may lead to uneven application across classrooms.

Policy should define clear expectations at a high level, while allowing educators to adapt implementation based on grade level, subject, and instructional goals.
